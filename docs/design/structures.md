# 3D structures

How each drug's complex is found, reviewed, built, served and drawn. Each choice says what was picked and why, with the issue and the evidence. The short version is under "Key design choices" in [Architecture](../architecture.md#key-design-choices).

## Finding and building the complexes

- **Complex candidates match the target's gene name in any species**, not the UniProt accession, because many reference structures aren't human (1IEP is mouse ABL1). A viral target is the exception: its gene names are generic (`pol` is HIV-1 and foamy virus), so the protein must come from the same virus species. HIV-1's `gag-pol` counts as `pol`, because many HIV-1 structures name only the polyprotein. Human entries rank first, then by resolution, and a person reviews the list ([complex candidates](../decisions/complex-candidates.md), #61, #70, #69, #83).
- **Hero complexes are reviewed by hand and precomputed.** Each shows the therapeutic target, not a transport protein: ibuprofen bound to albumin is not its mechanism. `data/hero_set.yaml` holds 73, picked by target, then human, then wild type, then resolution, and names the ligand copy, because the top-ranked candidate was often a resistant mutant, a chimera or the wrong site. A stand-in for the main target gets a caption, an animal version of it doesn't; prodrugs are entered in their active form ([hero complexes](../decisions/hero-complexes.md), #6). The ingest turns each into JSON, so the frontend never parses mmCIF (#7).
- **A complex keeps the protein, the nucleic acids, the hero ligand copy and the metal ions and cofactors it binds through**, not a list of waters, ions and buffers to drop, so a second drug in the entry goes too (tiamulin next to azithromycin in 8E42, diclofenac next to aspirin in 3N8Y). Modified residues stay: aspirin's acetylated Ser530. The pocket and the trace span every chain the ligand touches, so dimer and subunit-interface pockets and the DNA of the integrase and gyrase complexes are shown; a chain the hero review leaves out is listed in `exclude_chains` (losartan's nanobody, 9EAI chain B, is the one). Bonds come from biotite's bundled copy of the chem-comp dictionary by atom name and their orders from the chem-comp SMILES (RDKit `AssignBondOrdersFromTemplate`), because PDB coordinates have neither; a hydrogen the SMILES spells out to fix a C=N bond's geometry is removed first (zanamivir, metformin). Secondary structure is biotite's P-SEA, which needs only CA atoms; nucleotides are traced at their P atom without one. Contacts are each pocket residue's closest heavy-atom pair (≤ 4.0 Å) plus every N/O pair ≤ 3.3 Å as a hydrogen bond, in any chain. On 1IEP this reproduces the six published imatinib H-bonds for chain A and five for chain B (#126, #127, `ingest/complexes.py`, `docs/data-plan.md` §5.4).
- **A metal ion is kept when it is within 2.8 Å of a ligand atom; a cofactor only when `hero_set.yaml` lists it.** The drugs that bind through a metal sit 1.84 to 2.20 Å from it (zinc in 3HS4 and 1O86, both magnesium ions in 9C9M and 3OYA, the magnesium in 5BTC), while the nearest sodium ions of the transporter sites are 3.45 Å and farther (5I71, 9JKI, 8Y2G), so a distance separates bound from nearby. No distance separates a cofactor from a lipid or another ligand (warfarin's monoolein is 2.54 Å away, methotrexate's NADPH 3.27 Å), so the heme of 4WMZ and the NADPH of 1U72 are listed by hand. A cofactor's bonds and orders come from biotite's copy of the chem-comp dictionary, so it needs no chem-comp request and builds offline. Their contacts go into `contacts` with the pocket's, plus `coordination` for every metal within 2.8 Å of a ligand N, O or S; the tool lists a metal or cofactor with the residues (`ZN301`). An atom that binds a metal has no hydrogen or lone pair left for a hydrogen bond, so it isn't counted as one: this drops 14 N/O pairs that crossed a metal, the zinc-bound His94 and His96 in 3HS4, Glu411 in 1O86 and the integrase's Asp and Glu in 9C9M and 3OYA, and keeps heme's nitrogens out. Waters stay dropped, also the ones that bridge ciprofloxacin's magnesium to the enzyme (#144, `docs/data-plan.md` §5.4).
- **A complex file is named by the drug's CID** (`structures/complexes/{cid}.json`), like the conformers, so `get_complex(cid)` needs no table and no schema change, and a drug outside the hero set has no file and no complex, online or not (#127). The file carries the hero entry's `target` and `organism` next to its `caption` and `showcase`, so nothing reads `data/hero_set.yaml` at run time and a complex can't describe an entry the store wasn't built from (#109).

## Serving them

- **`GET /drugs/{cid}/complex` serves the stored complex file as it is**, plus `source_url`, the RCSB entry page, which the schema derives from the PDB ID so the files built before it need no rebuild. All 73 serve in 0.23 s together through the test client; a median of 56 KB, the largest 306 KB (azithromycin, 8E42). A supported drug without a complex gets a 404 that says so and that the 3D view shows the molecule alone, the same single reason `get_complex` gives (#73, #109).
- **The agent gets a summary of the complex, never the viewer JSON.** `get_complex` (`tools/get_complex.py`) returns the PDB ID, target, organism, ligand code, caption, the hydrogen bonds with their atoms and every pocket residue with its closest distance, and cites the RCSB entry page: a median of 1,258 characters over the 73 complexes, at most 2,472, against up to 306 KB of JSON for the view. A supported drug without a reviewed complex gets `no_complex`: every drug with a usable structure has one ([hero complexes](../decisions/hero-complexes.md)), so there is one reason, and the 3D view shows the molecule alone (#109). The answer then says just that, without describing the molecule: with nothing in the result to describe, gpt-oss-20b described acetaminophen's ring and groups from memory in 17 of 20 answers, 0 after the rule in the system prompt ([follow-up 3D eval](../evals/followup-3d-2026-10-18.md#the-molecule-alone-265-2026-10-19), #265).

## Drawing them

- **The 3D view sits in the evidence panel, drawn by 3Dmol.js bundled with the app** (`components/structure/`, `lib/complex-scene.ts`, #21, #303). A turn that looked up a structure (`get_complex`) has one view per drug, with Full screen; the step's "View in 3D" opens it. For #21 the owner picked an inline panel in a mockup with real complexes over a dialog opened from the step (hidden until opened) and a docked side panel (changes the page layout and still needs the dialog on phones), then moved it above the answer: under it, the streaming text pushed the view off screen. In the redesign prototypes (2026-10-19) the owner saw both again and picked the side panel, reversing #21: the panel shows one answer's view at a time, so views no longer stack up as the chat grows, and phones get it as a sheet. A view is built afresh, and plays its binding, each time an answer's 3D tab is shown. 3Dmol comes from npm and loads as its own chunk when a panel first appears, so the view works offline: showing imatinib, oseltamivir and acetaminophen, every request went to `localhost`. The complex JSON has one point per residue, so the protein is a trace line; ribbons need the whole backbone. A switch shows the drug alone, the binding site (the drug, the residues within 4 Å and the hydrogen bonds as dashed lines labelled by residue; the default) or that plus a faint backbone. The view is an instrument's screen (#297, the owner's pick in the redesign prototypes): dark in both themes (`#1B1D20` in light mode, `#0F1012` in dark) with a viewfinder's corner marks, the title, the switch and the buttons on it, and soft fades behind them where the protein reaches under the text. Since the screen is dark in both themes, the app has one palette for it (`COLORS.screen` in `components/structure/scene.ts`, which the legend's swatches also use; only the background is a shade darker in dark mode): lime drug carbons, orange hydrogen bonds, grey residues, light nitrogen, oxygen and sulfur, and the violet metals and cyan cofactors kept from before, checked on 3HS4 (zinc) and 4WMZ (heme). The printed picture (#312) keeps the earlier light colours as `COLORS.paper`, 3Dmol's own, with green drug carbons and grey residues, since it goes on white paper. The drug's carbons keep their colour in every view and for a drug without a complex, on screen and on paper (#318): until then they turned 3Dmol's light grey in Molecule view, for no recorded reason, so the drug changed colour when the view switched, and the legend's one lime dot named the drug while its nitrogens, oxygens and sulfurs showed blue, red and yellow. The legend now says the lime is the drug's carbons and shows the nitrogen, oxygen and sulfur colours, only those the view draws (the drug's, plus the pocket's outside Molecule view), in every view and in print; the owner pointed both out on acetazolamide. Since #322 the legend names every element the view draws besides carbon: hydrogen (a drug without a complex; the PubChem conformer has them, a PDB complex doesn't), chlorine, fluorine, iodine and phosphorus (the DNA and RNA in four pockets), in a molecular formula's order. Each palette writes out a colour for every element it names, so the view never falls back to 3Dmol's and the legend never guesses. 3Dmol's chlorine (`#00FF00`) read as one more lime carbon in the 28 drugs with chlorine, and its fluorine (`#DAA520`) sat close to sulfur's yellow, so on the screen chlorine is pink (`#F472B6`) and fluorine a bluer green than the carbons (`#34D399`), the two hues left free between the carbons, sulfur, the metals' violet and the cofactors' cyan. On paper chlorine is a darker pink (`#DB2777`), since the printed drug carbons are 3Dmol's `#00FF00` too, and hydrogen grey (`#B0B0B0`), since white doesn't show on paper; fluorine keeps the screen's green there, because a dark green looked like the heme's dark teal in the printed fluconazole picture (4WMZ). The owner picked pink chlorine over pink fluorine in a mockup with alprazolam, fluconazole, acetaminophen, levothyroxine and tenofovir disoproxil. The backbone (`#3A3E44`) is drawn solid, because at the earlier 35 % opacity it vanished on the dark screen. A readout in the corner counts the drug's atoms, the residues within 4 Å and the hydrogen-bond contacts from the complex JSON (37 · 23 · 6 for imatinib in 2HYY), and only the atoms for a drug without a complex. Hydrogens aren't counted, since a PDB structure seldom has them and a PubChem conformer has them all, so a drug gets the same count either way. The view turns slowly until it is grabbed, and not while it is off screen. The binding plays as a short sequence (#172), after the owner's three.js prototype but drawn with 3Dmol like the rest of the view: the drug builds itself up atom by atom 18 Å out of the pocket, the camera pulls back while the backbone grows along the chain, the drug moves and turns into its bound pose while the camera stays on it (the owner's call, because the drug is the subject), while it docks, Binding site fades the backbone out again and the residues within 4 Å grow in around the drug, nearest first; then each metal bond and hydrogen bond reaches out from the drug to its residue and its label fades in, one after the other. Until #306 the pocket and each bond popped in at once when docking ended; the owner picked this growing end over one where the pocket and the bonds fade in, after watching both in Chrome. The camera needed no change: it already lands on its end pose, measured in Chrome as no difference between the last docking frame and that pose. It plays when the panel is first half on screen and on "Replay binding"; leaving Molecule plays it without the build-up, and a drug without a complex only builds itself up. Each atom appears as a ball and gets its sticks once it is full size, because 3Dmol draws a bond only between two atoms that both have sticks. The backbone grows and fades in 20 and 5 steps, since each step restyles the whole protein: on the largest complex (azithromycin in the ribosome, 3,008 residues) Chrome drew the 8.3 s sequence with 2 frames over 50 ms. The pocket grows in on the backbone's 5 fade steps (#306), so Binding site restyles no more often than before: on the owner's AMD GPU (dev server, warm runs) it drew with no frame over 50 ms both before and after, and Whole protein, which gains those 5 steps, with each step at 45–50 ms. A click or drag in the view or Reset view jumps to the end. Only the end pose is from the structure; the path is an illustration. Reduced motion turns off the sequence and the turning. A drug without 3D (the two peptides) gets one line saying why, and a view that can't be drawn says so. Each view draws into its own box and a closed one gives back its WebGL context, since browsers keep only about 16 per page and drop the oldest for a new one. So a view more than two viewport heights off screen is closed too, and built again when it comes back, in its mode and with its camera, without playing the binding again: a long conversation keeps every view, and the wide margin keeps normal scrolling from rebuilding views (#175).
- **The metal ions and cofactors a drug binds through are drawn with the site** (`components/structure/scene.ts`, `lib/complex-scene.ts`, #267). A metal is a ball in the violet of its metal bonds, labelled with its element, and so is the metal inside a cofactor (heme's iron); the label sits centred 1.2 Å past the metal on the side away from the drug, and the binding site is framed on the drug and those metals. A cofactor is thin cyan sticks, so the drug stays the focus. The first version used Jmol's element colours, slate-grey cofactors and labels on the metals; in the live check the labels covered the 2 Å bonds of zinc and heme iron, magnesium's green read as part of the drug and NADPH as pocket residues (#271). A metal bond (`coordination`) is a dashed line like a hydrogen bond, in violet instead of amber, and the metal bonds appear before the hydrogen bonds. In the binding sequence the metals and cofactors appear while the backbone grows, before the drug docks, so it is seen moving onto its zinc or heme iron rather than next to an empty spot; the pocket's residues grow in while it docks. The legend lists "metal bond" and "cofactor" only for a complex that has them, and Molecule mode and the complexes without metals or cofactors look as before.
